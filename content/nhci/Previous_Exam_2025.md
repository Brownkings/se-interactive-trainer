### Question 1: SASSA Cashless ATM System Case Study & Questions [50 Marks]
**CASE STUDY: cashless ATMs to pay SASSA social grants**  
The South African Social Security Agency (SASSA) has been seriously impacted during the coronavirus pandemic in administering social grants. To safeguard vulnerable citizens from the month-end rush, the Agency implemented an alternating process of grant payments. Two payment files were created: one covering elderly and disability grants, and another covering child support, foster child, and care dependency grants. However, an anomaly left several recipients unable to obtain payment.  
The Agency resolved to invent other means to assist beneficiaries more effectively and efficiently. One solution was the ability to obtain grant payments at preferred retail outlets. Beneficiaries could change payment collection from the Post Office to their chosen bank account or vice versa.  
Another initiative was **cashless ATMs** rolled out to spaza shops, township and rural traders to make financial services available locally, tackle boundaries, and promote local economies.  
When a grant beneficiary reaches a dealer with a cashless ATM, they:  
1. Insert the SASSA card in the card slot and initiate a transaction using their PIN.  
2. Choose an amount to be deducted.  
3. If successful, the cashless ATM prints a merchant and customer slip approving the debit.  
4. The customer presents the merchant slip to the dealer, who hands out cash or goods equal to the withdrawal.  
Using the cashless ATM, users can also purchase pre-paid electricity/airtime, pay municipal bills, activate insurance, transfer money, and make balance enquiries.

# Suggested Solutions

### Question 1: SASSA Cashless ATM System Case Study & Solutions
* **1.1 Core questions for assumptions/claims (4 Marks)**:
  * Are there problems with an existing product or user experience? If so, what are they? (1)
  * Why do you think there are problems? (1)
  * What evidence do you have to support the existence of these problems? (1)
  * How do you think your proposed design ideas might overcome these problems? (1)
* **1.2 Previous UX (2 Marks)**:
  * Recipient experience was characterized by long queues and the risk of funds running out, resulting in beneficiaries being sent home without their allowance. (2)
* **1.3 SASSA Initiatives (4 Marks)**:
  * Alternating payment process using two distinct payment files (one for elderly/disabled, one for child support/foster care). (1)
  * Ability to collect grant payments at preferred retail outlets. (1)
  * Flexibility to switch collection mode between Post Office and bank account. (1)
  * Introduction of cashless ATMs at local spaza shops and traders. (1)
* **1.4 Crucial Step in Deliberating (2 Marks)**:
  * The project team should explicitly consider alternative solutions rather than immediately fixing on one idea. (2)
* **1.5 Advice on Generating Alternatives (4 Marks)**:
  * Seek cross-fertilization of ideas from different perspectives, individuals, and contexts. (2)
  * Observe the evolution of similar existing systems or websites through use. (1)
  * Straightforward copying or adapting of similar, existing solutions. (1)
* **1.6 Deciding Between Alternatives (4 Marks)**:
  * Letting users and stakeholders interact with prototype alternatives and discussing their experiences, preferences, and suggestions for improvement. (4)
* **1.7 Seeking Perspectives (2 Marks)**:
  * Prompt the designers' own experience, study others' ideas and suggestions, and deliberately seek out suitable sources of inspiration. (2)
* **1.8 Usability Goals (12 Marks, 2 Marks each)**:
  * *Effectiveness*: Beneficiaries successfully access their allowance without fail.
  * *Efficiency*: Beneficiaries can carry out non-cash transactions (e.g. buying airtime/electricity, balance checks) quickly.
  * *Safety*: Protect users from errors (e.g. no Delete/Quit buttons next to OK/Save, confirmation dialogs).
  * *Utility*: Provide the right functions (grant collection, bill payments, airtime, balance inquiries).
  * *Learnability*: Easy for diverse (including elderly) beneficiaries to learn to use without formal training.
  * *Memorability*: Easy to remember how to operate the ATM upon returning next month.
* **1.9 Accessibility & Inclusiveness (3 Marks)**:
  * Yes, they considered accessibility and inclusiveness by separating the elderly and disabled payments into a dedicated file. Catering to these vulnerable groups addresses accessibility, which inherently supports inclusiveness. (3)
* **1.10 Visibility & Affordance (6 Marks)**:
  * *Visibility*: Has visible physical controls (number keypad, backspace, OK) and screen prompts that guide the user on what to do next. Position of controls makes it easy to find them. (3)
  * *Affordance*: Interface buttons give physical cues for how to use them (e.g. keypad buttons invite pressing, backspace invites undoing errors, OK invites confirmation). (3)
* **1.11 Interaction Mode (3 Marks)**:
  * Mode: **Instructing** (1)
  * Definition/Example: Users carry out tasks by telling the system what to do. The ATM uses simple instruction buttons where the beneficiary presses buttons to execute actions. (2)
* **1.12 Conceptual Model (4 Marks)**:
  * The model is:
    1. Beneficiary inserts SASSA card and enters PIN at local dealer. (1)
    2. Beneficiary enters/deducts desired withdrawal amount. (1)
    3. ATM prints merchant and customer slip confirming the debit. (1)
    4. Customer presents slip to dealer to receive cash or goods. (1)
