# Chapter 2: User-Centered Approach

## What You Must Learn and Know

### 1. User-Centered Approach
* **User-Centered Design (UCD)** is an approach where real users and their goals, not just technology, are the driving force behind product development.
* **Objective**: A well-designed system should make the most of human skill and judgment, be directly relevant to the activity in hand, and support rather than constrain the user.

### 2. Gould and Lewis' Three Principles
John Gould and Clayton Lewis (1985) laid down three principles that lead to a "useful and easy to use computer system":
1. **Early Focus on Users and Tasks**: Observing users doing their normal tasks, studying the nature of those tasks, and involving users directly in the design process.
2. **Empirical Measurement**: Early in development, observing and measuring user reactions and performance to scenarios, manuals, and low-fidelity prototypes.
3. **Iterative Design**: When problems are found in user testing, they are fixed, and then more tests and observations are carried out to see the effects of the fixes.

### 3. Four Basic Activities of Interaction Design
1. **Establishing Requirements**: Identifying users and stakeholders, and determining what features and qualities the system must possess.
2. **Designing Alternatives**: Proposing conceptual models and interface layouts. Alternatives come from seeking ideas, cross-disciplinary flair, and researching existing designs.
3. **Prototyping**: Developing interactive representations of the design, ranging from paper wireframes to digital mockups.
4. **Evaluating**: Assessing the usability and user experience of the system. Evaluation does not replace quality assurance, but focuses on human performance.

### 4. Who are Users and Stakeholders?
* **Users**: People who interact directly with the product (e.g., students booking tutors).
* **Stakeholders**: Anyone affected by the system, including direct users, indirect users (e.g., parents paying for sessions), managers, administrators, and developers.

### 5. Lifecycle Models
* Interaction design is inherently **iterative**. Lifecycle models show how the four basic activities are linked and repeat over time. Feedback from evaluation leads to re-evaluating requirements or redesigning alternatives.

## Key Terms and Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **User-Centered Design** | An approach to design that grounds the process in information about the people who will use the product. |
| **Empirical Measurement** | Observing and measuring user performance and reactions during design testing. |
| **Iterative Design** | A design methodology based on a cyclic process of prototyping, testing, analyzing, and refining a product. |
| **Stakeholder** | Any individual or group who has an interest in or is affected by the outcome of a software system. |

## Practice Test

### Section A: Multiple-Choice Questions
1. Which of the following is NOT one of the three principles of design laid down by Gould and Lewis?
   a) Early focus on users and tasks
   b) Empirical measurement
   c) Iterative design
   d) Cost-benefit analysis

2. Which interaction design activity involves generating potential conceptual models and interface layouts?
   a) Establishing Requirements
   b) Designing Alternatives
   c) Prototyping
   d) Evaluating

3. An indirect stakeholder in an online student tutoring system would most likely be:
   a) The student booking the session
   b) The tutor conducting the session
   c) The parent paying for the student's tuition
   d) The system administrator

4. What is the primary purpose of Iterative Design?
   a) To reduce the number of developers needed
   b) To fix usability problems found in user testing and test the fixes
   c) To document the software architecture in detail
   d) To replace the need for database backups

### Section B: Short-Answer & Scenario Questions
5. Explain Gould and Lewis's three principles of user-centered design.
6. What are the four basic activities of interaction design?
7. Differentiate between a direct user and a stakeholder.

## Answer Key

### Section A Solutions
1. **d) Cost-benefit analysis**. Gould and Lewis's three principles are early focus on users/tasks, empirical measurement, and iterative design.
2. **b) Designing Alternatives**. Proposing different concepts, navigation flows, and mockups is the core of designing alternatives.
3. **c) The parent paying for the student's tuition**. They do not use the system directly but are affected by it and have an interest in its success, making them a stakeholder.
4. **b) To fix usability problems found in user testing and test the fixes**. Iteration allows design refinement based on real user feedback.

### Section B Solutions
5. Gould and Lewis's three principles:
   * **Early focus on users and tasks**: Understanding who the users will be by observing them in their normal work environments and involving them in design.
   * **Empirical measurement**: Testing early versions (simulations, manuals, paper mockups) with users to measure performance and capture reactions.
   * **Iterative design**: Cyclic process where test findings are used to refine designs, followed by further testing to verify fixes.
6. The four basic activities are:
   * **Establishing Requirements**: Identifying target users, tasks, and system constraints.
   * **Designing Alternatives**: Creating conceptual models and user interface designs.
   * **Prototyping**: Creating interactive mockups to explore and test design ideas.
   * **Evaluating**: Assessing the design's usability and user satisfaction.
7. A **direct user** is someone who directly interacts with the software interface to achieve a task (e.g., student using booking app). A **stakeholder** is a broader term encompassing anyone who has an interest in the product or is impacted by it, including direct users, indirect users, managers, and sponsors.
