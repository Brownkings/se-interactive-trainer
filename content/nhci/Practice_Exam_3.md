# Suggested Solutions

### Question 1: Interaction Design Foundations & Solutions
* **1.1 Usable meaning**: Developing products that are in general easy to learn, effective, and offer a pleasant user experience. (2 Marks)
* **1.2 Key factors to consider**:
  * Who is going to be using the products. (1 Mark)
  * How the products are going to be used. (1 Mark)
  * Where the products are going to be used. (1 Mark)
* **1.3 Impact of not understanding daily activities**: This will affect the design of the interface because the precision of various styles of interfaces and the arrangement of input and output devices is determined by the kind of activities that are to be supported. (4 Marks)
* **1.4 Definition of interaction design**: Designing interactive products to assist in ways that people communicate and relate in their daily and working lives. (2 Marks)
* **1.5 UX as UXD**: This is to inspire design thinking that concentrates on user experience quality rather than on the design methods that are set to be used. (2 Marks)
* **1.6 Keyboard behavior comparison**:
  * Keyboard covering text field (Image A) is not usable because the keyboard covers the focused text field causing the user to type without seeing the text. (2 Marks)
  * Content moving up (Image B) is usable because the focused content moves up when the keyboard is visible so the user can see their input. (2 Marks)
* **1.7 Accessibility vs Inclusiveness**:
  * Accessibility denotes the degree to which an interactive product is accessible by as many users as possible. (1 Mark)
  * Inclusive design is where designers strive to make their products and services support the broadest potential number of users. (1 Mark)
* **1.8 Safety Evaluation**:
  * Usability goal not considered: **Safety** (not protecting the user from performing an undesirable action). (2 Marks)
  * Three weaknesses:
    1. The delete button and the save button are placed next to each other. (1 Mark)
    2. The delete button is given the most visual importance/prominence using larger text and a bright red color. (1 Mark)
    3. The interface does not provide the user with a means to recover should they make errors (such as an undo button or confirmation dialog). (1 Mark)

### Question 2: SPU Website Improvement Solutions
* **2.1 Intern's incorrect approach**: In creating an interactive product, it can be tempting to start working out how to design the physical interface and what technologies and interaction styles to use. Starting here is incorrect because the university's potential users and their context can be misunderstood, and the website's usability and user experience goals can be overlooked. (4 Marks)
* **2.2 Importance of articulating problem space first**: In articulating the nature of the website's problem space, the team will better understand what the current user experience is, why a change is needed, and how this change will improve the user experience. (3 Marks)
* **2.3 Design Approach**: The **Genius approach** because it relies mainly on the experience and creative flair of its design team. (2 Marks)
* **2.4 User involvement & role**: Yes, they will be involved. Their role is to validate ideas generated by the design team, however, they are not involved during the design process itself. (2 Marks)
* **2.5 Importance of user involvement**: It is the best way that the design team can ensure that the university website is usable and that it indeed will be used after deployment. (2 Marks)
* **2.6 Target users**: Students, lecturers, marketing team, or external stakeholders. (Any two, 1 Mark each) (2 Marks)
* **2.7 Why target users**: Target users are users who perform the activity every day or who will use the intended product on a regular basis. (2 Marks)
* **2.8 Non-functional aspects**:
  * **Expectation management**: The process of making sure that the users' expectations of the website are realistic, ensuring there are no surprises for users when the website is rolled out or deployed. (2 Marks)
  * **Ownership**: University website users who are involved and feel that they have contributed to a website's development are more likely to feel a sense of ownership toward it and support its use. (2 Marks)
